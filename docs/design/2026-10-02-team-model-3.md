# Team model 3: the teams a soul may join, and its default, are committed in the workspace

**Status:** decided and implemented (kernel 0.38.0, feature `team-model-3`; prepared in 0.36.1).
Specs: awebai/oats#484 and #485, agreed by both maintainers on 2026-10-02. This record supersedes the
membership and default-team parts of [team model v2](2026-09-27-team-model-v2.md); v2's shared teams,
unmapped ids, live teams and provider environment stand. The reference pages
([workspaces.md](../workspaces.md#teams), [capabilities.md](../capabilities.md#teams-in-the-provider-environment),
[desktop-cli-api.md](../desktop-cli-api.md#team-model-3-feature-team-model-3-oats-0370-replaces-feature-team-model-2))
win on operator-visible behaviour.

## Why

**The local bridge.** An instance that sits in two teams is a bridge between them: one process reads
both inboxes, holds both contexts in one session, and can relay anything from one to the other. Under
team model v2, one person's `oats-local.yaml` could put an organisation's instance into a team the
organisation never sees: a local extra team in `souls.teams`, or a local default team while its souls
joined the organisation's shared teams. Either way it never showed in the organisation's git, and it
differed per person and per machine. The organisation owns its teams' trust boundary, so the teams its
instances can be in are its decision, committed in its workspace, and closed by default.

**Scale.** With many souls and teams, per-deployment lists were repeated by every person on every
machine.

**Principle**, consistent with v2's "shared facts committed, personal choices local": everything
workspace-specific lives in the workspace file; souls stay team-free in their repositories, open source
included; an organisation says how souls behave in its teams in its own `oats-workspace.yaml`, and
someone running the souls standalone sees none of it.

## What it protects against, and what it does not

The workspace's eligibility rules (`souls:`, `localTeams` closed by default) **prevent accidental joins**
in a cooperative installation, and make the organisation's intended team set visible and reviewable in
its git. That is all they claim.

They are not anti-bridging enforcement, and neither is the messaging layer. Distinct keys do not prove
distinct processes: an operator controlling two identities can read under one and relay under the
other, or copy content outside the messaging layer. aweb admission controls who holds credentials for
a team (the team controller key signs memberships; hosted invites are mediated by the service), not
what a process does with what it reads. A deployment's authority comes from the credentials it holds,
not from its path or its name. Today's team certificates carry no soul, workspace or home-team claim,
and any such metadata would be self-asserted, so it would not be prevention. Enforcement on the
messaging side (admission per root, claims in certificates, a namespace policy, bridge visibility for a
team's owner) is a separate question for the aweb protocol owners.

## The model

### `oats-workspace.yaml`

```yaml
teams:                                   # shared teams, as in v2
  engineering: { team: "engineering:acme.aweb.ai" }
  security:    { team: "security:acme.aweb.ai" }
  docs:        { team: "docs:acme.aweb.ai" }
defaultTeam: engineering                 # the workspace's fallback default team (a shared label)
localTeams: false                        # may deployments declare their own teams? (absent: false)
souls:                                   # per pattern: the default team and the other teams a soul may join
  "*": { teams: [] }
  security-souls/*: { default: security, teams: [engineering] }
  security-souls/incident-responder: { default: security, teams: [engineering, docs] }
  oats.engineering/*: { teams: any }
```

- **Keys are patterns:** `<member>/<soul>` or `<package>/<soul>`, `<member>/*` or `<package>/*`, and
  `"*"`, with the names `souls.disabled` already uses. A soul's key is its qualified name. Bare names are
  refused: a bare name is ambiguous across members, and the qualified form is what the workspace sees.
- **The most specific key wins outright for teams,** with no merging across levels; the default comes
  from the most specific key that sets one. `a/*: {default: security}` and `a/x: {teams: [docs]}` give
  `a/x` the default `security` and the teams `[docs]` only. Merging was rejected: an entry that adds to
  a broader one hides what a soul can join behind two places, and the point of the model is that a
  reviewer reads one entry.
- **`any`** is every shared team of the file.
- **A soul no key matches** (and no `"*"`) has its default only, member and package souls alike; a
  workspace opens a package explicitly.
- **Every label is a shared team of the same file.** An unknown label, or a default that isn't one, is
  `E_WORKSPACE_SCHEMA` when the file is read, never at a spawn on someone else's machine. A key that is
  neither a pattern nor a discovered soul's qualified name is the warning `team-soul-unknown` (a typo
  guard; not an error, because the file is validated without discovery and packages come and go).

### The default team, in order

1. the soul's `default` from `souls:` (`from: "soul"`);
2. else the deployment's `oats-local.yaml` `defaultTeam`, only when `localTeams: true`
   (`from: "deployment"`);
3. else the workspace's `defaultTeam` (`from: "workspace"`);
4. else none (`E_TEAM_UNCONFIGURED` when a messaging layer is active).

The soul's own default comes first because it is the organisation's most specific statement; a
personal default, where allowed, comes before the workspace's fallback so a person can keep their own
default team (awebai/oats sets `localTeams: true` for this).

### The teams a soul may join

Its default, plus the `teams` of its most specific matching key, plus, with `localTeams: true`, every
local team the deployment declares (there is no per-soul local list: a deployment that is trusted with
local teams is trusted with them for every soul). Each row says why: `via` ⊂ `default`, `workspace`,
`local`. Unchanged from v2: an instance joins only its default at spawn; the others are offered and
joined on request, and the messaging provider decides admission from the eligible rows it receives in
`OATS_TEAMS`.

### `oats-local.yaml`

- `souls.teams` and `souls.default` are removed keys. The refusal names each key found and prints the
  `souls:` that gives the same result (a v2 soul's teams were its default ∪ `"*"` ∪ its own list, so each
  named soul's entry lists `"*"`'s teams too; a bare soul name is written `<member>/<soul>` for the
  operator to qualify), so a deployment's migration is a copy, a qualification and a PR.
- `teams` and `defaultTeam` are allowed only when the workspace says `localTeams: true`. Otherwise every
  spawn, preview and inspect refuses with `E_WORKSPACE_SCHEMA` (reason `local-teams-closed`), and `oats
  teams`, readiness and doctor report it as a failure. The message names both fixes: allow local teams,
  or commit them in the workspace and remove them locally. The rule can only be checked where the
  workspace file has been read, so it is not part of `oats-local.yaml`'s own validation.
- The standalone view (explicit, or a fallback when the workspace can't be read) has no workspace rules:
  it is an individual running souls outside any organisation, so local teams apply.
- Verbs: `oats teams add` and `oats teams default` refuse where local teams are closed; `oats teams
  remove` runs there, since removing local teams is the last step of committing them in the workspace.
  `oats soul teams` is read only; its edit flags were removed (a soul's teams are a PR to the workspace).

### `soul.yaml` and `oats-membership.yaml`

Unchanged: they say nothing about teams, so a soul in a public repository carries no organisation's
labels. Per-soul lists in `oats-membership.yaml` and teams in `soul.yaml` were rejected for the same
reason v2 rejected them: labels belong to a workspace, and there should be one central place.

## Reporting (contract)

- `defaultTeam.from` (spawn preview, `inspect`, `oats souls`, readiness, `OATS_DEFAULT_TEAM_FROM`):
  `soul`, `deployment` or `workspace`. `soul` changed meaning: it was the local `souls.default`.
- Every `TeamRow` gains `via`.
- `oats teams --json` is `teamsApi: 2`: `localTeams`, a `DefaultTeam` for the deployment, and the
  workspace's `souls:` in place of the local `souls` block. `oats soul teams --json` is
  `soulTeamsApi: 2`: `match` and `defaultMatch` (the `souls:` keys that applied) in place of `local` and
  `all`. Feature `team-model-3` replaces `team-model-2`.
- Readiness: local-teams-closed (failure) and `team-soul-unknown` (warning) join the team items;
  `E_TEAM_NOT_ELIGIBLE` is no longer a kernel item (a soul's default is always one of its teams); it
  remains the provider's join refusal.

## 0.36.x → 0.38.0

0.36.1 accepted and validated `defaultTeam`, `localTeams` and `souls:` without applying them, and warned
(`team-model-3-migration`) about what 0.38.0 refuses, so every workspace could commit its side first, as
0.29.4 did for 0.30. 0.38.0 applies the keys, refuses the old shape, and retires the warning. The steps
for each kind of deployment are in the [0.38.0 release notes](../release-notes/v0.38.0.md).

## Out of scope

Enforcement on the aweb side (above); nested teams; the Desktop's team-editing UI (its own spec:
anything that writes `souls.teams` or `souls.default` goes).
